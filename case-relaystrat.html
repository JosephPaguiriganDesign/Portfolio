Software for the Space Tasking Cycle, designed in partnership with U.S. Space Force operators and strategists. User research and usability testing weren't optional — they were the job. No stock photo UI here.

DoD / Space Force Mission Software Operator Research Classified Workflows Section 31
Ethos · 01

Operators first.

Every screen co-designed with the people who'd use it on a console. Weekly hands-on usability tests, not a single throwaway persona.

Ethos · 02

Mission scale.

From a single sensor MX entry to a multi-payload launch schedule, the same component system holds together. Designed to scale with the cycle.

Ethos · 03

Classification-aware.

Markings, paragraph portion marks, releasability rails. The interface treats classification as a first-class data type — not an afterthought.

Ethos · 04

Built to ship.

Shipping software to active operators on real timelines. No stock-photo UI, no theoretical workflows — only patterns that survive a watch shift.

01  ·  The Approach

Field
over theory.

This wasn't enterprise software with a side of compliance. It was compliance-first software where every interaction was tested against a console operator's reality — and rejected if it didn't survive it.

Phase 01 · Ride-along

Sit at the console first.

Before sketching a single screen, we shadowed strategists writing SODs by hand and operators receiving them. We mapped the artifact's full lifecycle — draft, review, publish, distribute, revise — with the people who lived it.

If you haven't seen the watch floor at 0300, you can't design for it.
Phase 02 · Co-design

Whiteboards > mockups.

Weekly sessions with Space Force operators and strategists. We didn't bring polished comps to validate — we brought rough flows to break. The orange rail, classification markings, and section nav all came out of those rooms.

First time I've seen a designer change the workflow mid-meeting.
Phase 03 · Survive a shift

Tested where it ships.

Usability testing wasn't a lab moderated study. It was operators running a real SOD draft through Relay Strat on a real console, on a real day. If a pattern didn't survive a shift, it didn't ship.

Saved me an hour on the first draft. Didn't have to ask anyone how to do anything.

Callout · 01
Section tree, always anchored.

Left rail mirrors SOD doctrine. Section state surfaced as colored dots so authors find their unfinished work.

Callout · 02
Doc state, three pills.

Draft · Preview · Published replace ambiguous "Final v3 (revised) FINAL.docx" filenames. State is system, not folklore.

Callout · 03
Tables, not paragraphs.

Schedules & rosters that strategists used to type into paragraphs became structured data — sortable, filterable, exportable.

Callout · 04
Lock + Review as one move.

Single CTA collapses two anxious steps. Locking implies review-ready; review-ready implies locked. No half-states.

04  ·  The Testing

If it didn't survive
a shift, it didn't ship.

Eight rounds of moderated tests on the watch floor and over secure VTC. Each round produced a findings log scored for severity and confidence, and a redesign pass that shipped to the next round — no patterns went forward unless operators confirmed them.

F-014
Operators couldn't distinguish "draft saved" from "submitted for review." Six of eight clicked Save and walked away. R2 · 8 participants · CFSCC J3, J5
Critical
7/8
Killed the Save button entirely. Autosave on every blur; "Lock & Review" is now the only deliberate state change. Re-tested R3: 8/8 correct.
F-022
Hand-typed (S//REL FVEY) markings drifted from paragraph rules in 4 of 12 sampled SODs. Editorial-only audit caught two; two shipped. R3 · audit of 12 historical SODs
Critical
12/12
Classification became a chip data type. The portion mark, REL TO, and export behavior travel together. Operators can't type their way into a mismarked document.
F-031
Reviewers waded through full SODs to find their two paragraphs. Average time-to-comment: 14 min on R2. R3 · 6 reviewers timed
High
6/6
Added per-section ownership + per-paragraph review queue. Time-to-comment dropped to 3.5 min on R4. Confirmed on R5 & R6.
F-047
Tasked units couldn't filter the published SOD to "my section." Three of four scrolled the entire doc. R5 · 4 operators · USSPACECOM watch
High
3/4
Added the right-rail filter: Tasked Units, Op Objectives, Intel Requirements. Operators land on their slice without reading the whole document.
F-058
Activity feed scrolled with the document body — comments were "lost" when reviewers paged down. R6 · 5 reviewers
Med
5/5
Floated the Activity / Collaborators panel with sticky tabs. Comments and presence persist as the doc scrolls.
F-066
"DOWNLOAD" was the most-clicked button in Published view — operators preferred the offline PDF over the live doc. R7 · click-tracking · 22 operators
Med
19/22
Promoted DOWNLOAD to a primary CTA in Published state. Added per-section anchor links so the PDF is no longer a workaround — it's a first-class output.
Round 2 · failed

Save · Submit · Approve

Three buttons. Operators didn't know which committed the SOD, and a "submitted" draft could still be silently edited.
"I clicked Save. It looked like it sent. It hadn't." — CFSCC strategist, R2
Round 4 · shipped

Autosave + Lock & Review

One deliberate action. Locking is the same act as opening for review — ambiguity gone, and operators can't accidentally edit a doc that's already out.
"I know exactly what state the doc is in. Either I'm editing, or it's out." — CFSCC strategist, R4
