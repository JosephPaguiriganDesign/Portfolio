The most critical workflow in HR, buried in its own complexity.

HireRight screens candidates for 40,000+ organizations. But the tool recruiters lived in had grown into a maze. Order statuses overlapped and meant different things to different people. Candidate reports were so dense no one could scan them. The experience drifted from region to region. The work was mission-critical, and the interface was working against it.

Every candidate, every status, one screen.

Recruiters live here. A real-time list of every background check in flight, filterable, sortable, exportable, with status pills that mean the same thing whether you're hiring in Dallas or Düsseldorf.

Orders and Reports

Six states, globally consistent.

The old system had 27 status strings across regions. We collapsed them to six clear states, In progress, Awaiting consent, Needs review, Flagged, Complete, Cancelled, with a single color language that survives translation.

Updated, not refreshed.

Behind the dashboard, a websocket bus pushes status changes within seconds of upstream vendors completing a check. Recruiters stop refreshing; the table just updates.

Built for power users.

Every recruiter has a "Mine, urgent" view. Operations has "Stalled > 5 days." Legal has "Flagged, North America." Filters compose into saved URLs they paste into Slack.

Compliance-dense. Decision-ready.

A background report is one of the densest documents in HR, criminal history, education, identity, credit, drug testing, motor-vehicle records. The redesign organizes it so a hiring manager can read it in two minutes, and adjudicators can act in five.

Decision first, data underneath.

A red "Needs review" headline pill, the count of clear/flagged checks, then the dense detail. Hiring managers scan; adjudicators read; everyone gets what they need.

Every event, timestamped.

Right-side activity feed shows the order's full life, consent, vendor calls, returns, escalations. The same audit log that compliance officers used to dig out of database exports.

Rules transparent, never hidden.

When the rules engine recommends "Engage," it shows its work, which policy, what thresholds. Adjudicators retain full FCRA-compliant override authority.

Hiring at a glance, compliance under the hood.

Insights pulls every order, every adjudication, every vendor SLA into one place, so HR leaders can spot regional drift, slow vendors, or hiring bottlenecks before they cost a quarter.

Same product, on the train.

A recruiter on the go needs to triage, not order. Mobile collapses to the few moves that matter: see what needs attention, see one candidate, kick off the next screen.

The 12 items needing me, ordered by SLA. Tap a row → full report. Pull to refresh; nothing else.

The full report compressed to what matters on a phone: status, counts, what to do next.

Four steps to a screening: candidate, package, consent, review. Region rules apply themselves; the recruiter just answers.

Colophon

Type. Big Shoulders Display, optically kerned, for the broken word. Newsreader for the text, with old-style figures in prose and lining figures in the index. Small caps for the labels. Fraunces italic for the proof notes.

Grid. A 24px baseline runs under the page. Twelve columns. The collision field steps off that grid on purpose, then the case list locks back onto it.

Ink. Black on proof paper. One accent, a press red, for the overprint, the strikes, and the notes. Cyan, magenta, yellow, and black appear only in the color bar.

Build. Static HTML and CSS. No framework. The grain is an SVG noise filter, not a photograph.
