Real tools for real missions.

Software for the Space Tasking Cycle, designed in partnership with U.S. Space Force operators and strategists. User research and usability testing weren't optional, they were the job. No stock photo UI here.

Operators first.

Every screen co-designed with the people who'd use it on a console. Weekly hands-on usability tests, not a single throwaway persona.

Mission scale.

From a single sensor MX entry to a multi-payload launch schedule, the same component system holds together. Designed to scale with the cycle.

Classification-aware.

Markings, paragraph portion marks, releasability rails. The interface treats classification as a first-class data type, not an afterthought.

Built to ship.

Shipping software to active operators on real timelines. No stock-photo UI, no theoretical workflows, only patterns that survive a watch shift.

Field over theory.

This wasn't enterprise software with a side of compliance. It was compliance-first software where every interaction was tested against a console operator's reality, and rejected if it didn't survive it.

Sit at the console first.

Before sketching a single screen, we shadowed strategists writing SODs by hand and operators receiving them. We mapped the artifact's full lifecycle, draft, review, publish, distribute, revise, with the people who lived it.

Whiteboards > mockups.

Weekly sessions with Space Force operators and strategists. We didn't bring polished comps to validate, we brought rough flows to break. The orange rail, classification markings, and section nav all came out of those rooms.

Tested where it ships.

Usability testing wasn't a lab moderated study. It was operators running a real SOD draft through Relay Strat on a real console, on a real day. If a pattern didn't survive a shift, it didn't ship.

Before the first pixel.

Strategists were drafting SODs in Word docs, marking up classification by hand, and emailing PDFs for review. We mapped the full lifecycle of the artifact, from blank cursor to published & distributed at echelon, with the people who lived it.

SOD lifecycle, current state, summer 2020

Whiteboard to shipped.

Three rough sketches set the spine of the product, an editable section tree, classification chips inline with content, and a per-paragraph review state. The hi-fi wireframe locked the layout. Every UI element you see in the final composition traces back to a marker on these boards.

Mid-fi wireframe, SOD authoring view

Left rail mirrors SOD doctrine. Section state surfaced as colored dots so authors find their unfinished work.

Draft · Preview · Published replace ambiguous "Final v3 (revised) FINAL.docx" filenames. State is system, not folklore.

Schedules & rosters that strategists used to type into paragraphs became structured data, sortable, filterable, exportable.

Single CTA collapses two anxious steps. Locking implies review-ready; review-ready implies locked. No half-states.

If it didn't survive a shift, it didn't ship.

Eight rounds of moderated tests on the watch floor and over secure VTC. Each round produced a findings log scored for severity and confidence, and a redesign pass that shipped to the next round, no patterns went forward unless operators confirmed them.

Save · Submit · Approve

Autosave + Lock & Review

By R4, every operator could tell a saved draft from a submitted one at a glance. Time-to-comment dropped from 14 minutes to under 4, reviewers went straight to their own paragraphs instead of scrolling whole documents, and the offline-PDF workaround became a first-class export.

The real thing, in production.

Relay Strat, live across the CFSCC mission set since 2022.
